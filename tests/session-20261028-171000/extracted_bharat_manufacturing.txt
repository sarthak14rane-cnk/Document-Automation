INTRODUCTION

We, Messrs. CNK & Associates LLP, Chartered Accountants, (“CNK”) are pleased to present for DPDP Impact Assessment & Compliance review for Bharat Manufacturing Industries Limited.
The proposal summarizes the benefit of Digital Personal Data Protection Impact Assessment and Compliance for an organization like yours , with our standard Terms of Reference. 
Entity Coverage
This engagement will cover the following 2 entities:
Bharat Steel Limited
Bharat Logistics LLP
Bharat Manufacturing Industries Limited, together with Bharat Steel Limited and Bharat Logistics LLP, holds personal data across the employment and operational lifecycle: recruitment, payroll and biometric attendance records, contractor and driver identities, plant safety and training logs, and customer and vendor contacts maintained in ERP, CRM and factory systems. The logistics arm further captures GPS-linked driver and shipment data, while shared services consolidate records across the three entities for group reporting.For the DPDP assessment, each dataset would be mapped to a specified purpose and matched with notice and consent (or deemed-consent) mechanisms, retention schedules and erasure rules. The review would also test Data Principal rights and grievance redressal, breach notification readiness, processor agreements with payroll and telematics vendors, and the security safeguards governing transfers between the parent and its subsidiaries.
What is Personal Data for Bharat Manufacturing Industries Limited
Based on our understanding of Bharat Manufacturing Industries Limited's activities and programs, we believe that the organization processes personal data across multiple operational, programme, healthcare, donor and support functions. The nature of personal data may include the following:
Employee & Worker Data
Steel plants and equipment workshops employ permanent staff and contract labour whose HR, payroll and statutory records are processed across the group entities.
Name, address, contact details, family and nominee information
PF, ESI, PAN, Aadhaar and bank account for payroll
Joining, attendance, leave, shift and performance records
Contract and contractor-labour deployment records (Bharat Steel Limited)
Exit, gratuity and full-and-final settlement data
Occupational Health & Safety Data
Steel manufacturing involves hazardous processes, requiring medical surveillance, fitness and incident records for workers under FACTORIES and OSH obligations.
Periodic medical examination and fitness certificates
Health records for workers in hazardous processes (heat, fumes, noise)
Accident, injury and near-miss investigation reports
PPE issue and safety-training / competency certifications
Blood, vision and hearing test results linked to individuals
Site Access & Biometric Data
Plant and warehouse premises use gated entry with ID proof, photographs and biometric or card-based attendance capture.
Gate entry logs, visitor identity proof and photographs
Biometric templates or RFID card attendance records
CCTV footage of plant, warehouse and logistics yard areas
Vehicle entry and driver log registers
Contractor and vendor employee on-site access passes
Customer & Buyer Data
Sales of steel products and industrial equipment involve B2B buyer contacts, order records and post-sale service interactions across the group.
Contact person name, designation, phone and email of buyers
Quotations, orders, invoices and delivery instructions
Credit limits, payment history and outstanding dues
Complaint, warranty and after-sales service requests
Site delivery addresses and installation contact details
Supplier & Contractor Data
Raw material, scrap, logistics and job-work vendors supply contact and compliance data of their nominated individuals and deployed workers.
Vendor proprietor and authorised signatory details
Vendor employee and labour deployment records at plants
KYC, GST, PAN and bank details of vendor contacts
Tender, bid and contract negotiation correspondence
Vehicle and driver details for inbound material movement
Logistics & Transport Data
Bharat Logistics LLP moves steel and equipment, processing driver, transporter and consignee personal data across shipments.
Driver licence, vehicle and transporter contact details
Consignee receiver name, phone and delivery address
Shipment proof-of-delivery signatures and photographs
E-way bill, route and stop-level movement records
Customer-side delivery coordination and appointment logs
Website & Digital User Data
The corporate website and enquiry forms collect visitor contact details and usage data for marketing and communication purposes.
Contact form name, email, phone and enquiry message
Newsletter and marketing communication preferences
IP address, device, cookie and analytics identifiers
Job application and career-page submissions
Consent and preference records under DPDP notice requirements
Recruitment & Professional Documents
Hiring for technical and plant roles generates candidate data including resumes, identity proofs and background verification records.
Resume, qualifications and employment history
References and background verification report outputs
Interview notes, assessment and selection decisions
Identity and address proof documents collected for verification
Candidate communication and offer acceptance records
Data Processing Characteristics under DPDPA
Bharat's data processing ecosystem may involve:
Employee personal data, including identity details, payroll information, and attendance records, is collected directly from employees and used for statutory compliance, salary administration, welfare administration, and workforce management under DPDP notice and consent obligations.
Worker data from contractors, vendors, and site visitors, including Aadhaar or government identifiers, is collected for access control, site safety, gate management, and contractual labour compliance, with processing limited to specified purposes.
CCTV footage, biometric logs, and badging records are collected at plant perimeters, production floors, and warehouses for security, safety monitoring, incident investigation, and access control, subject to documented retention schedules and restricted access.
Customer, dealer, and supplier contact and KYC data is collected through contracts, tenders, and correspondence and processed for order fulfilment, invoicing, credit evaluation, logistics coordination, and regulatory filings.
Health, disability, and occupational injury data of employees is processed for workers' compensation, ESI and PF compliance, occupational health surveillance, and workplace accommodation, with access limited to authorised HR and medical personnel.
Personal data is shared with statutory authorities, auditors, banks, insurers, logistics providers, and IT and cloud vendors under contractual safeguards, reflecting fiduciary obligations regarding Data Principal notice, consent, and grievance redressal.
Personal data is stored across on-premises systems and cloud platforms operated in India, and cross-location access by group entities Bharat Steel Limited and Bharat Logistics LLP is governed by intra-group agreements and access controls.
Retention periods are defined per record category, aligned with labour, tax, and environmental statutes; upon expiry, data is archived, anonymised, or securely deleted, and Data Principals may request correction, erasure, or grievance resolution.
DPDPA Applicability and Key Obligations
Based on the nature of its operations and processing of personal data relating to Bharat's beneficiaries, patients, employees, donors, volunteers, partners and other stakeholders, Bharat Manufacturing Industries Limited would need to assess and implement the applicable obligations under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP Act). The applicability and extent of specific obligations would depend on Bharat's processing activities, role as Data Fiduciary or Data Processor, and other factors prescribed under the applicable legal framework.
1. Lawful Processing & Consent Management
Establish appropriate lawful grounds for processing personal data, including consent where required.
Provide clear and accessible privacy notices to data principals, as applicable.
Obtain consent in a manner that is specific, informed and capable of being withdrawn where consent is the applicable basis.
Maintain appropriate records and evidence of consent and related processing activities.
Establish mechanisms to facilitate withdrawal of consent where applicable.
2. Purpose Limitation & Data Minimization
Collect and process personal data only for specified and legitimate purposes related to service delivery, programme implementation, monitoring, reporting and organisational administration.
Limit collection to personal data that is necessary for the intended purpose.
Review existing forms, databases and data collection channels to identify unnecessary or excessive data collection.
Ensure that subsequent use or sharing of personal data remains aligned with the applicable purpose and legal requirements.
3. Data Principal Rights Management
Enable data principals to exercise their rights:
Right to access information about personal data being processed.
Right to correction and updating of personal data.
Right to erasure of personal data, subject to applicable legal and retention requirements.
Right to grievance redressal.
Right to nominate an individual to exercise rights in accordance with applicable provisions.
Appropriate channels, ownership and turnaround mechanisms should be established for receiving, authenticating, tracking and responding to such requests.
4. Data Security Safeguards
Implement appropriate technical and organisational measures to protect personal data.
Apply appropriate access controls and role-based access based on business need.
Protect personal data during storage and transmission.
Implement appropriate authentication, encryption, endpoint and network security controls, as applicable.
Maintain audit trails, security logs and monitoring mechanisms for relevant systems.
Periodically review access privileges and security controls, particularly for systems containing sensitive or health-related information.
5. Personal Data Breach Management
Establish a formal personal data breach identification, escalation and response framework.
Define roles and responsibilities for identifying, assessing, containing and remediating personal data breaches.
Establish mechanisms for notification to the Data Protection Board and affected data principals, where required under applicable law.
Conduct root-cause analysis and corrective actions following significant incidents.
Maintain appropriate records of incidents, response actions and remediation.
6. Data Retention & Record Management
Define appropriate retention periods for personal data based on applicable legal, contractual, operational and organisational requirements.
Establish a formal data retention and disposal policy.
Periodically review stored personal data to identify information that has exceeded its applicable retention period.
Implement secure deletion, disposal or anonymisation, as appropriate, once retention requirements have been fulfilled.
Ensure that archived data remains subject to appropriate security and access controls.
7. Vendor & Third-Party Risk Management
Identify third parties and service providers that process personal data on behalf of the organisation, including partners, technology providers, cloud service providers, consultants and other outsourced providers, as applicable.
Define appropriate contractual requirements relating to data protection, confidentiality, security and breach management.
Conduct appropriate privacy and information-security due diligence of relevant third parties.
Establish mechanisms to monitor third-party compliance with applicable data protection requirements.
Ensure appropriate controls over data sharing and onward processing by third parties.
8. Additional Obligations (if classified as SDF)
Depending on the applicability of specific provisions and any future classification or notification applicable to the organisation, additional governance measures may be required. These may include:
Strengthening the organisation's privacy governance framework and accountability structure.
Periodic assessment of personal data processing activities and associated privacy risks.
Implementation of privacy-by-design principles for new programmes, systems and technology initiatives.
Periodic review of privacy policies, notices, consent mechanisms and data processing practices.
Maintaining appropriate records, documentation and evidence of compliance.
Conducting periodic privacy and data protection reviews/audits, where applicable.
Enhancing employee and stakeholder privacy awareness and training.

OBJECTIVE 

Bharat Manufacturing Industries Limited proposes to undertake a Digital Personal Data Protection Impact Assessment and compliance assessment, covering its key business processes, IT infrastructure, applications, programmes and third-party ecosystem involved in the collection, processing, storage, sharing, archival and deletion of personal data.
The objective of this exercise is to assess Bharat's existing data protection practices, identify compliance gaps and associated privacy risks, and recommend appropriate remediation measures to align its data governance, contractual arrangements, IT controls and personal data lifecycle management with the applicable requirements of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP Act).
To support Bharat Manufacturing Industries Limited in its privacy and compliance journey, CNK will assess the existing data governance framework with focus on:
Distributed data collection across beneficiaries, patients, employees, donors, partners and programme activities.
Consent and privacy notice practices across offline and digital data collection channels.
Multi-stakeholder data sharing, including healthcare institutions, programme partners, donors and external service providers.
Personal and health-related data retention and archival practices.
Third-party and Data Processor management and contractual data protection requirements.
Organisation-wide DPDPA compliance across relevant functions and programmes.
Privacy-by-design considerations for business processes, programmes and technology initiatives.
Risk assessment to evaluate the likelihood and potential impact of identified personal data privacy and breach risks.
Development of appropriate mitigation measures and remediation actions to address identified personal data risks and compliance gaps.

Scope Of Work

This initiative will follow a phased implementation approach for Bharat Manufacturing Industries Limited, covering Data Discovery and Mapping, Risk Assessment, Policy & Process Development, Technology Enablement, Capacity Building and Compliance Monitoring.
Based on our discussions and experience in the area of data protection and privacy, the scope will focus on assessing Bharat's personal data processing activities across its beneficiary and patient programs, employees, donors, partners, healthcare institutions, vendors and supporting business functions.
The scope of work is indicative and may be further refined based on the findings emerging during the assessment. Any additional area identified during the engagement that is relevant to Bharat's DPDPA compliance and data protection objectives may also be considered as part of the engagement.
The entire engagement will be divided into four phases, covering the assessment of the current state, identification and prioritization of gaps and risks, development of the required privacy framework and controls, and support towards implementation and ongoing monitoring.
Phase 1 DPDP Impact Assessment (Current State Review)
The objective of Phase 1 is to establish a clear understanding of Bharat's personal data landscape, processing activities, technology environment and existing privacy controls, and to identify key gaps and risks against the applicable requirements of the DPDP Act.
The assessment will include:
Review and inventory of existing personal data collection points, sources and methods, including beneficiary/patient interactions, outreach programs, events, websites, forms, email and other relevant channels.
Assessment of personal data shared with partner hospitals, healthcare institutions, program partners, donors, vendors, consultants and other external parties, as applicable.
Review of personal data processed in connection with healthcare, program delivery, monitoring, reporting, financial, employment and other applicable legal or contractual requirements.
Review of personal data processed through websites, digital platforms, online forms, communication channels and other technology systems.
Review of methods, policies and controls relating to storage, accessibility, internal sharing, processing and use of personal data.
Review of existing personal data retention, archival and deletion practices.
Review of communications and information shared with external stakeholders and third parties by relevant departments and personnel.
Assessment of the extent, nature and purpose of personal data disclosed or transferred outside the organisation.
Assess the applicability of the DPDP Act and identify key compliance risks across relevant business, program and IT operations.
Conduct personal data discovery and data-flow mapping across relevant functions and technology platforms to understand how personal data is collected, processed, stored, transferred and deleted.
Identify high-risk processing activities, including processing of health-related, identification and other potentially sensitive personal information.
Map Data Principal touchpoints across beneficiary/patient interactions, outreach programs, websites, forms, communication channels, employee interactions and other relevant points of data collection.
Conduct discussions/interviews with relevant personnel to understand what personal data is collected, the purpose of collection, where it is stored, who has access to it, with whom it is shared and how long it is retained.
Perform a detailed gap assessment against applicable DPDP Act and Rules requirements and identify areas requiring remediation or improvement.
Assess the applicability of DPDP requirements across the organisation's various functions, programs and operational units, including relevant legal and regulatory considerations.
Provide inputs on Data Fiduciary/Data Processor roles, territorial applicability, processing purposes and sector-specific considerations, wherever applicable.
Review the system and application landscape, including interfaces with third-party platforms, healthcare partners and other external systems.
Assess the current IT and data environment, including infrastructure, applications, interfaces, access controls and overall data landscape.
Review relevant contracts and agreements with employees, vendors, healthcare institutions, program partners, consultants, technology providers and other service providers.
Review existing cybersecurity and data protection controls against applicable regulatory requirements and recognized industry practices.
Review business continuity and disaster recovery arrangements relating to the availability, backup and archival of personal data.
Evaluate existing IT governance, privacy governance, controls, ownership and reporting mechanisms.
Key Deliverables
Personal Data Inventory across relevant functions and programmes.
Personal Data Flow Diagrams (DFDs) for identified business/programme units.
DPDPA Gap Assessment Report covering applicable personal data processing activities, systems, controls and third-party ecosystem.

Phase 2a   Governance, Policy & Compliance Framework Design

The objective of Phase 2A is to establish a practical and sustainable data protection governance framework for Bharat Manufacturing Industries Limited, aligned with applicable DPDP requirements and the organisation's operational, healthcare, programme and stakeholder ecosystem.

The scope will include:

Define the DPDP governance framework and target operating model, including roles, responsibilities, accountability and escalation mechanisms.
Define appropriate privacy/data protection ownership, including roles, responsibilities, reporting structure and operating procedures for the designated privacy function.
Identify and develop DPDP-aligned policies, privacy notices, standards and SOPs applicable to the organisation.
Develop guidelines and processes covering personal data collection, consent/notice, retention, storage, access, sharing, processing and secure deletion.
Establish a vendor and third-party privacy assessment framework, including appropriate Data Processing Agreement (DPA) requirements.
Recommend DPDP-aligned contractual safeguards and model data protection clauses for relevant employees, vendors, partners, healthcare institutions and service providers.
Define record-keeping and documentation requirements to support ongoing DPDP compliance and accountability.
Prepare function/programme-level privacy guidelines, including practical Do's and Don'ts for personnel handling personal data.
Provide guidance on:
Personal data obtained from third-party sources and program partners.
Personal data obtained from public sources or the open internet, where applicable.
Sharing of employee and stakeholder personal data for legitimate business or program activities.

Phase 2b   Privacy Controls, Technology & Operational Design
The objective of Phase 2B is to translate the governance and policy framework into practical privacy, security and operational controls that can be implemented across the organisation's programmes, functions, technology environment and third-party ecosystem.

The scope will include:
Design a consent and privacy notice framework, including consent lifecycle, withdrawal mechanisms, record retention and deletion processes, wherever consent is the applicable basis for processing.
Evaluate appropriate consent models for different functions and stakeholder categories, including beneficiaries/patients, employees, donors, volunteers and other relevant Data Principals.
Assess requirements for consent management tools or technology enablement, where applicable.
Design privacy and security controls aligned with applicable DPDP requirements and the organisation's operating environment.
Assess technology dependencies, system vulnerabilities and existing control gaps, and recommend required privacy and security enhancements.
Develop a structured DPDP implementation plan and compliance roadmap, including prioritised remediation activities.
Conduct a high-level review of technology architecture, applications, data repositories and system interfaces from a data protection and privacy perspective.
Design a personal data breach management framework, covering identification, escalation, incident response, notification and reporting requirements.
Develop a privacy risk mitigation and control enhancement roadmap based on the risks identified during the assessment.
Define appropriate controls for managing consent, notices and personal data processing across different stakeholder categories.
Provide guidance on privacy requirements for the exchange of reports, programme information, beneficiary information and other personal data through email, WhatsApp and other digital communication platforms.
Support the integration of privacy, cybersecurity and operational controls into the organisation's overall DPDP implementation plan.
Recommend appropriate mechanisms for privacy monitoring, control testing and periodic compliance review.

ASSUMPTIONS, LIMITATIONS & SCOPE EXCLUSIONS

Limitations
The core engagement will comprise three phases, covering assessment, framework design and implementation/rollout. Any ongoing Phase 4 – Sustain/Operate activities will be separately discussed and agreed based on Bharat's requirements.

Annexure 1-Definitions 

Data Fiduciary -An entity that decides why and how personal data is processed, either alone or with others.
Data Principal -The individual to whom the personal data relates. In the case of a child, this includes a parent or lawful guardian. For a person with a disability who cannot act independently, this includes the lawful guardian acting on their behalf.
Data Processor- Any entity that processes personal data on behalf of a Data Fiduciary.
Consent Manager - An entity that provides a single, transparent and interoperable platform through which a Data Principal may give, manage, review or withdraw consent.

Annexure 2- Key penalties under the DPDP Act (as of the 2025 Rules)

Up to ₹250 Crore: Failure to implement reasonable security safeguards to prevent data breaches.
Up to ₹200 Crore: Failure to notify the Board or affected individuals regarding a personal data breach.
Up to ₹200 Crore: Non-compliance with obligations regarding children's data, including tracking or behavioral monitoring.
Up to ₹150 Crore: Non-compliance by a Significant Data Fiduciary (e.g., failure to appoint a Data Protection Officer or conduct audits).
Up to ₹50 Crore: Any other violation of the Act or Rules, such as not obtaining valid consent or failure to handle data principal rights.
Up to ₹10,000: Failure of a Data Principal to comply with their duties (e.g., impersonation or filing false complaints)
